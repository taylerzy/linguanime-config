# Outreach Email Standard (Taylor, Oct 11, 2026)

Every outreach email written for Taylor follows this. It replaces the Oct 10 drafting
brief's template wording wherever the two disagree. Taylor rejected the Oct 10 drafts as
sloppy; the rules below exist so that never happens again.

## Taylor's Requirements (his words)
- Polished, trustworthy, clear, honest, concise. Never spammy.
- Completely customized for each recipient, with research where the list is thin.
- A real transition: the first lines say why Taylor is writing to them.
- Persuasive, with the risk taken off them.
- A working link to taylorsfleet.com in every email.
- No blanks and no brackets anywhere. If something only Taylor knows is needed, ask him
  before drafting, never fill it with a placeholder or a generic stand-in.
- Done in rounds with a tracker, not in one pass.

## Process (every round)
1. Research each recipient first: the person who reads that inbox (owner, founder,
   manager, host profile name, show host) and one or two true, specific facts about them.
   Record a source link for the name and for every fact.
2. Samples first: for a new campaign or a new kind of recipient, write 5 to 8 samples and
   get Taylor's approval of the voice before writing at scale.
3. Write in rounds of about 25, tracked in the outreach tracker.
4. Second reviewer: a separate pass reads each email as the recipient and rejects anything
   generic, repeated, untrue, unclear or pushy. A script also checks every rule below.
5. Nothing reaches Taylor's mailbox until it passes both checks. Drafts load in one batch,
   never one tool call per draft.
6. Never send. Taylor decides when and how sending is scheduled. Each email is a one-off:
   no automated follow-ups; Taylor follows up personally only when they reply.
7. Drafts go to taylor@taylorsfleet.com (moving to Google Workspace, Oct 12, 2026), not to
   Taylor's personal Gmail.

## Email Shape (about 70 to 120 words above the signature)
Every email covers the things a stranger wants to know when it lands. If one is missing, the
email is babble: rewrite it. Cover them in whatever order reads naturally for that email,
not in a fixed slot.
1. Subject: Title Case, 3 to 6 words, names their business or their problem. No brackets,
   no "Re:", no all caps, no clickbait.
2. Greeting: "Hi {First}," when research ties a real person to that inbox; otherwise
   "Hi {Company} team,". Never "Hi there,".
3. Where Taylor found them, said the way a person would ("I read the Hawaii Business story",
   "your co-hosting page came up when I was looking at..."). Never claim to have read,
   watched or used something research did not find.
4. One specific, true thing about them, said plainly. One fact, not a list of their stats.
5. Why that matters to them: the consequence, in plain words, not a polished one-liner.
6. What the app does about it: Taylor hosts on Turo in Dayton and built the app for his own
   cars; then only the one or two outcomes that answer point 5, from Verified Claims. In
   first person ("I built an app"), not the product's full name. No feature lists.
7. The ask (Taylor, Oct 12, 2026): to try the app. Never a call, a meeting or a video,
   since Taylor can't call 100+ people a day. One question, plus the safety net in a few
   words (two weeks free, no card). The price is on the site; leave it out unless the email
   needs it. Partners (Taylor, Oct 12, 2026, later): for someone who reaches many hosts
   (co-host networks, Turo coaches and courses, host communities, VA firms, vendors that
   serve hosts), the email may also offer a partnership, ideally after asking them to try
   the app first: 25% of the profit from every host they bring, for as long as that host
   stays a customer (Taylor's terms, Oct 12, 2026), or for a VA firm a free fleet for its
   own team. Still one question, and never a call or meeting.
8. No opt-out line of any kind (Taylor, Oct 11, 2026). These are one-off, individually
   tailored emails that Taylor follows up on personally, and only when they reply.

The reviewer's test: read only the first two sentences. If they don't say who they are to
Taylor and why he is writing, the email fails.

## Sound Like Taylor, Not a Template (Taylor, Oct 12, 2026)
Taylor rejected the Oct 11 samples because they read as AI-written. Every round, check for
and remove these tells:
- The same opening in more than one email ("I came across X while looking at Y").
- Grading the reader ("is a serious run", "a smart way to grow", "impressive").
- Reciting several of their own numbers back to them. One fact is enough.
- Announcing the purpose ("that's why I'm writing", "I'm writing because").
- Neat one-liner zingers that sum up their problem.
- The same self-introduction, feature sentence or ask in more than one email of a round.
  Vary the words, not the facts: "I'm a Turo host in Dayton", "I host about a dozen cars
  on Turo in Dayton", "I host on Turo myself, in Dayton".
- App jargon the reader doesn't use ("clean-by time"); say "a deadline from the car's next
  booking" or similar.
- Every email the same shape and length.

The side-by-side test: put any two emails of a round next to each other. If they look like
the same template with different names, both fail.

## Verified Claims (say only these, in plain words)
- When a trip ends, cleaners are asked by email one after another in the order you set,
  until someone says yes. No app or account for the cleaner. Say "when a trip ends", not
  "the moment": a first request can wait until morning when nothing is due soon.
- Each car's clean-by time comes from its next booking (or 9 AM the next morning if
  sooner); a car running late is flagged while there is still time.
- Turo's payout emails are read as they arrive: earned, paid out, still owed.
- Real profit per car: trip income less cleaning, repairs, tires and other costs.
- Shop invoices (PDF or phone photo) are read and filed to the car they name.
- Cleaner payments recorded; year-end totals and the 1099-NEC export ready.
- Bookings come from Turo's trip emails (Gmail read-only or a forwarding rule); private and
  direct bookings are added from the Trips tab.
- Trip photo zips for damage claims: the cleaner's photos from before pickup, originals with
  time stamps.
- Mechanic dispatch with a runner; the car's cleaning is called off.
- A VA login that sees no money (a co-host login sees everything, including the money); one
  login can work in several fleets.
- From $15 a car a month ($15 for cars 1 to 5, $12 for cars 6 to 20, $10 from car 21); 14 days
  free; no card to sign up; the first payment is refunded on a cancel within 30 days of it. In
  an email say "from $15 a car a month", never a flat $15.
- Partners: 25% of the profit from every host they bring, for as long as that host stays a
  customer (Taylor, Oct 12, 2026: "25% of all lifetime profits for each customer"; profit is
  what that host pays, minus card fees and refunds); VA firms
  get a free fleet for their own team. Say "25% of the profit", never "25% of what they
  pay". A first email to a partner may offer these (see Email Shape, point 7);
  to anyone else, only when they reply.
- A booking typed in by hand is an ordinary trip: it gets the same deadline and cleaner
  emails as a Turo trip.

## Never
- Invent a fact, name, number, post, episode or relationship.
- Say it texts or pays cleaners, messages guests, changes listings or prices, or signs in to
  a customer's Turo account.
- Quote customer counts, ratings or testimonials.
- Mention lists, research tools or other recipients. (Saying where Taylor came across them
  is required: see Email Shape.)
- Exclamation marks, emoji, bold, buzzwords, more than one question.
- "Hi there,", lowercase subjects, "Reply stop" or any opt-out line, brackets,
  placeholders, working markers.
- Any link other than taylorsfleet.com and Taylor's LinkedIn.
